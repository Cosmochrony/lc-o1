  \begin{abstract}
    We address LC-O1, the reconstruction of effective Lorentz transformations between
    inertial observers from projective temporal ordering data, and close it conditionally on a supplied co-metric
    and on one named hypothesis.
    Under a quadratic Born--Infeld budget hypothesis and the identification (S) of TempProj, LorCap and TempProj
    give $F^\tau = \sqrt{1-\beta^2} = 1/\gamma$
    for the normalised spatial capacity load $\beta$.
    No source constructs the needed Lorentzian co-metric; we take a supplied co-metric of the conditional form of
    Q11, $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, 2\lambda, 2\lambda, 2\lambda)$ with two independent coefficients, proportional to
    the Minkowski form $\eta$ after rescaling the ordering coordinate; $g^{\mu\nu}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}$ is a choice of
    units.
    Identifying the capacity load with the velocity of an inertial observer, a unit timelike splitting of $\eta$,
    requires a named hypothesis, [H-cone]: the
    light-cone speed of the co-metric equals the Born--Infeld bound per ordering step.
    It is the coupling datum that Q11 leaves open, and it is not derived here.
    Under it, given two splittings with relative capacity load $\beta$, the unique proper
    orthochronous isometry of the tangent interval that maps one temporal axis
    to the other and fixes the transverse directions is the standard Lorentz boost $\Lambda(\beta)$, and length
    contraction $\ell = \ell_0/\gamma$ follows on the simultaneity
    hyperplane of the moving splitting.
    The boost and contraction formulae are proved for $\eta$; their reading as the observer transformations of the
    capacity data is conditional on the supplied co-metric and on [H-cone].
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
    \label{sec:introduction}

    The Lorentz-capacity sub-programme studies relativistic kinematics from the bounded
    Born--Infeld capacity budget of admissible projection on
    $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
    Paper~\cite{Beau2026n} identifies the projective temporal capacity $F^\tau = \sqrt{1-B^2}$
    as the Lorentz factor residual, and~\cite{Beau2026tp} proves that the temporal residual map
    \[
      P^{Q5b}_\tau \Sigma^{\mathrm{tot}}_n = \sqrt{1-B_n^2}
    \]
    is the unique positive function compatible with the Lorentzian splitting of a supplied co-metric, the
    Born--Infeld unit capacity sphere, and the Weil filling law, under the hypotheses stated there.
    Under [H-cone] (Section~\ref{sec:splittings}) this gives time dilation (the proper time of an observer at load
    $\beta$ advances at the rate $1/\gamma$ relative to the rescaled ordering coordinate) and the light
    cone ($B = 1 \Rightarrow F^\tau = 0$).

    The open problem LC-O1 is listed in Section~7.2 of~\cite{Beau2026tp}:
    length contraction requires the reconstruction of effective Lorentz transformations
    between inertial observers from projective temporal ordering data.

    The resolution rests on a strict separation of two structures.
    The Born--Infeld capacity sphere
    \[
      (F^\tau)^2 + \beta^2 = 1
    \]
    is a Euclidean capacity relation.
    It parameterises admissible temporal splittings and determines $\beta$ and $1/\gamma$,
    but it is not the Minkowski interval.
    The Lorentzian metric is a separate, supplied input: a co-metric of the conditional form of Q11,
    proportional to $\eta$ after a rescaling of $\tau$ (Section~\ref{sec:splittings}).
    Lorentz transformations are the isometries of $\eta$, not symmetries of the BI sphere.
    The BI sphere determines which splittings are admissible; the Lorentz group determines
    how to pass between them.

    Section~\ref{sec:splittings} defines inertial observers as unit timelike splittings of
    the effective metric and identifies the rapidity with the projective capacity load.
    Section~\ref{sec:boost} proves that the unique interval-preserving isometry mapping
    one splitting to another and fixing the transverse directions is the Lorentz boost.
    Section~\ref{sec:contraction} derives length contraction.
    Section~\ref{sec:pto} states the connection to projective temporal ordering.
    Sections~\ref{sec:discussion} and~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarise the logical structure.

  \section{Inertial splittings and capacity load}
    \label{sec:splittings}

    Let $\Meff = \mathbb{R}_\tau \times \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ carry a supplied homogeneous rank-four
    co-metric.
    No source constructs it: under the spatial-limit hypothesis [H-L] and the lifting hypothesis [H-lift],
    Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} gives a co-metric of rank three, and its rank-four extension is open problem Q5b-O2.
    We take the form of Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11}, Corollary~6.1,
    \[
      g^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau,\, 2\lambda,\, 2\lambda,\, 2\lambda),
      \qquad A_\tau > 0,\ \lambda > 0,
    \]
    which holds under the hypotheses of that corollary: [H-L] and [H-lift], a supplied full-rank extension
    satisfying [T] and [INV$^+$] of Q8~\cite{BeauQ8}, and invariance of its principal symbol under the action
    [ACT$_\tau$] with a negative ordering term.
    Three statements are distinct.
    The invariant form has two independent coefficients: Q8 leaves $\lambda$ free, Q10~\cite{Beau2026q10} derives
    no coefficient value, and Q11 shows that spatial $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ invariance leaves $A_\tau$ free.
    The signature $(-,+,+,+)$ is conditional on the hypotheses above.
    The rescaling $\hat\tau = \tau\,(2\lambda/A_\tau)^{1/2}$ gives $g^{\mu\nu} = 2\lambda\,\eta^{\mu\nu}$ with
    \[
      \eta = \mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1),
    \]
    and $\lambda = 1$ gives the normalisation $g^{\mu\nu} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}$; both are choices of units, not
    derived values.
    In the coordinate $\hat\tau$, written $\tau$ from now on (primes denote boosted coordinates),
    the isometry group of $g$ is $\SO(3,1)$, the same as that
    of $\eta$, whatever the value of $\lambda$.

    \begin{definition}[Inertial splitting]
      \label{def:inertial-splitting}
      An inertial observer is a future-directed unit timelike vector $u \in T_x\Meff$
      satisfying $\eta(u,u) = -1$, together with its orthogonal simultaneity hyperplane
      \[
        \Sigma_u := \bigl\{ X \in T_x\Meff \mid \eta(X,u) = 0 \bigr\}.
      \]
    \end{definition}

    \begin{remark}[Capacity sphere versus Lorentzian structure]
      \label{rem:bi-vs-lorentz}
      The Born--Infeld capacity sphere $(F^\tau)^2 + \beta^2 = 1$ is a Euclidean norm
      constraint on the capacity flow vector, not a Lorentzian structure.
      It provides the values of $\beta$ and $1/\gamma$ from projective data, but it does not
      determine the Lorentzian interval.
      The Lorentzian interval is supplied by $\eta$, from the supplied co-metric of the conditional form of
      Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11}.
      The Lorentz group acts as isometries of $\eta$; it does not act as isometries of the
      BI sphere.
    \end{remark}

    The rest observer corresponds to the splitting $u_0 = \partial_\tau$.
    An observer with normalised spatial capacity load $\beta$ in the $x$-direction has
    splitting
    \[
      u_\beta = \gamma(\partial_\tau + \beta\,\partial_x),
      \qquad
      \gamma := \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}.
    \]
    One verifies directly that $u_\beta$ is unit timelike:
    $\eta(u_\beta, u_\beta) = -\gamma^2 + \gamma^2\beta^2 = -1$.

    The velocity parameter $\beta$ of $u_\beta$ is measured in units of the light-cone speed of $\eta$.
    The capacity load of~\cite{Beau2026n,Beau2026tp} is normalised differently, by the Born--Infeld bound
    $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ per depth step, and satisfies, under the hypotheses of~\cite{Beau2026tp},
    \[
      F^\tau = \sqrt{1-\beta^2} = \frac{1}{\gamma}.
    \]
    Using the same letter for both requires the following hypothesis.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item[{[H-cone]}] \emph{Cone--capacity identification.}
      The light-cone speed $(2\lambda/A_\tau)^{1/2}$ of the co-metric equals the Born--Infeld bound per ordering
      step; equivalently, in the rescaled coordinate $\tau$, the normalised capacity load is the coordinate
      velocity of the splitting it labels.
    \end{itemize}
    [H-cone] is the datum coupling the temporal and spatial normalisations that Q11 names as missing (open problem
    Q11-O1; Q11 lists a saturation rule for the capacity bound among the candidate sources).
    It is not derived here or in any source.
    Under [H-cone], the rapidity of the inertial splitting is determined by the projective temporal capacity
    of~\cite{Beau2026tp}.

  \section{Boost reconstruction}
    \label{sec:boost}

    Let $\xi$ be the rapidity defined by
    \[
      \beta = \tanh\xi, \qquad \gamma = \cosh\xi, \qquad \gamma\beta = \sinh\xi.
    \]
    Then $u_\beta = \cosh\xi\,\partial_\tau + \sinh\xi\,\partial_x$.

    \begin{lemma}[Uniqueness of the inertial boost]
      \label{lem:boost-uniqueness}
      There is a unique proper orthochronous linear isometry of $(T_x\Meff, \eta)$ that
      maps $u_0 = \partial_\tau$ to $u_\beta$ and acts as the identity on the transverse
      directions $\partial_y, \partial_z$.
      It is the Lorentz boost of rapidity $\xi$, acting on the $(\tau,x)$-plane by
      \[
        \begin{pmatrix} \partial_{\tau'} \\ \partial_{x'} \end{pmatrix}
        =
        \begin{pmatrix} \cosh\xi & \sinh\xi \\ \sinh\xi & \cosh\xi \end{pmatrix}
        \begin{pmatrix} \partial_\tau \\ \partial_x \end{pmatrix}.
      \]
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      A proper orthochronous linear isometry of $\eta$ preserving $\partial_y$ and
      $\partial_z$ acts only on the $(\tau,x)$-plane.
      Preservation of the restriction $-d\tau^2 + dx^2$ requires a hyperbolic rotation:
      the isometry is of the form displayed above for some $\xi$.
      The condition $\partial_{\tau'} = u_\beta$ uniquely fixes
      $\cosh\xi = \gamma$ and $\sinh\xi = \gamma\beta$,
      hence $\xi = \mathrm{arctanh}\,\beta$.
    \end{proof}

    In linear coordinates on $T_x\Meff$ adapted to the frame, the boost reads
    \[
      \tau' = \gamma(\tau - \beta x), \qquad
      x'   = \gamma(x  - \beta\tau), \qquad
      y'   = y,                      \qquad
      z'   = z.
    \]
    These are statements on the tangent space $T_x\Meff$.
    A constant co-metric in the left-invariant frame of $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ is not
    flat, so reading the boost as a global change of inertial coordinates would require a supplied flat model, which
    is not constructed here.

    \begin{theorem}[Lorentz transformations from projective temporal ordering]
      \label{thm:lorentz-from-pto}
      Assume a supplied co-metric of the form of Q11 Corollary~6.1, written in the rescaled coordinate $\tau$, and
      \textup{[H-cone]}.
      Given a projective temporal capacity load $\beta$ determined by~\cite{Beau2026n,Beau2026tp},
      the unique proper orthochronous linear isometry of $T_x\Meff$ between the corresponding inertial
      splittings that fixes the transverse directions is the Lorentz boost
      \[
        \tau' = \gamma(\tau - \beta x),\qquad
        x'   = \gamma(x  - \beta\tau),\qquad
        y'   = y,\qquad z' = z,
      \]
      with $\gamma = (1-\beta^2)^{-1/2}$.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      The projective temporal capacity result of~\cite{Beau2026n,Beau2026tp} gives
      $F^\tau = (1-\beta^2)^{1/2} = 1/\gamma$, and [H-cone] makes $\beta$ the velocity of $u_\beta$, fixing
      $\xi$.
      In the rescaled coordinate the co-metric is $2\lambda\,\eta^{\mu\nu}$.
      Since the factor $2\lambda$ is a global constant rescaling, the isometry group of $g$ is
      $\SO(3,1)$, the isometry group of $\eta$.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:boost-uniqueness}, the unique proper orthochronous isometry of $\eta$
      mapping $\partial_\tau$ to $u_\beta$ and fixing the transverse directions is the
      displayed Lorentz boost.
    \end{proof}

  \section{Length contraction}
    \label{sec:contraction}

    \begin{corollary}[Length contraction]
      \label{cor:length-contraction}
      A rod of proper length $\ell_0$ at rest in the unprimed splitting has length
      $\ell = \ell_0/\gamma$ when measured on the simultaneity hyperplane of the primed
      splitting.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      Let the rod endpoints in the rest frame be $A = (\tau_A, x_A)$ and
      $B = (\tau_B, x_B)$ with $\Delta x = x_B - x_A = \ell_0$.
      The moving observer measures length at equal primed time, $\Delta\tau' = 0$.
      The boost gives $\Delta\tau' = \gamma(\Delta\tau - \beta\Delta x)$,
      so simultaneous in the primed frame means $\Delta\tau = \beta\Delta x$.
      The primed spatial separation is then
      \[
        \Delta x'
        = \gamma(\Delta x - \beta\Delta\tau)
        = \gamma(1-\beta^2)\Delta x
        = \frac{\Delta x}{\gamma}
        = \frac{\ell_0}{\gamma}.
      \]
    \end{proof}

  \section{Connection to projective temporal ordering}
    \label{sec:pto}

    The projective temporal ordering observable~\cite{Beau2026pto} supplies a monotone ordering proxy; it supplies no
    step and no coefficient.
    The clock-factor reading $F^\tau = 1/\gamma$ is that of TempProj~\cite{Beau2026tp}, as a proper-time rate
    under [H-cone].
    LC-O1 shows that this factor is not an isolated time-dilation effect.
    Once an effective Lorentzian interval is supplied and [H-cone] is assumed, the same capacity load $\beta$
    determines the rapidity $\xi = \mathrm{arctanh}\,\beta$ and hence the complete inertial
    transformation.

    The capacity data of LorCap and TempProj determine the clock axis of the observer.
    A supplied co-metric of the conditional form of Q11, rescaled in $\tau$, gives the Lorentzian interval on which
    this axis lives, and [H-cone] places the capacity load on its light cone scale.
    The boost is the unique interval-preserving change of admissible inertial splitting that fixes the transverse
    directions.

  \section{Discussion}
    \label{sec:discussion}

    The construction separates three layers, each with a distinct source.

    \textit{Layer 1: Born--Infeld capacity sphere.}
    The quadratic Born--Infeld budget, a hypothesis of~\cite{Beau2026n} expressing the capacity bound [A-cap]
    of~\cite{Beau2026c}, gives the unit capacity relation
    \[
      (F^\tau)^2 + \beta^2 = 1.
    \]
    This is a Euclidean norm constraint on the capacity flow vector.
    It is not the Minkowski interval.
    It provides $\beta$ and $1/\gamma$ from projective data.

    \textit{Layer 2: Effective Lorentzian co-metric.}
    A supplied co-metric of the form of Q11 Corollary~6.1~\cite{Beau2026q11},
    $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, 2\lambda, 2\lambda, 2\lambda)$ with two independent coefficients, becomes
    $2\lambda\,\eta^{\mu\nu}$ after rescaling $\tau$.
    This supplies the interval whose isometry group is $\SO(3,1)$.
    Neither the factor $2\lambda$ nor the normalisation $g^{\mu\nu} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}$ affects the isometry group.

    \textit{Link between Layers 1 and 2: [H-cone].}
    The capacity load of Layer~1 is normalised by $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, the velocity of Layer~2 by the light-cone
    speed.
    Their identification is the hypothesis [H-cone].

    \textit{Layer 3: Inertial observer and boost.}
    An inertial observer is a unit timelike splitting of $\eta$.
    Under [H-cone], the rapidity of a splitting is fixed by the projective capacity load $\beta$ from
    Layer~1.
    The Lorentz boost is the unique isometry of $\eta$ (Layer~2) that maps one admissible
    splitting to another at capacity load $\beta$ and fixes the transverse directions.

    Length contraction is a consequence of this boost, not an independent capacity law.
    It follows from the difference in simultaneity hyperplanes between two inertial
    splittings, given the interval structure of Layer~2.

    \begin{remark}[Logical status]
      \label{rem:logical-status}
      The inputs are conditional.
      The residual $F^\tau = 1/\gamma$ is proved in~\cite{Beau2026n,Beau2026tp} under the quadratic budget
      hypothesis and the identifications stated there.
      The co-metric is supplied, of the form of Q11 Corollary~6.1~\cite{Beau2026q11} under the hypotheses of that
      corollary, with two independent coefficients; its normalisation is a choice of units.
      The quadratic budget is a hypothesis expressing the bound [A-cap] of~\cite{Beau2026c}.
      LC-O1 adds the identification of inertial observers with unit timelike splittings, the hypothesis [H-cone],
      and the standard uniqueness of isometries of $\eta$ in each rapidity class.
      Lemma~\ref{lem:boost-uniqueness} and the boost and contraction formulae are proved for $\eta$; their reading
      as observer transformations of the capacity data rests on [H-cone], which no source derives.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    LC-O1 is closed conditionally.
    The capacity data of LorCap and TempProj determine the capacity load $\beta$ and the residual
    $F^\tau = 1/\gamma$.
    A supplied co-metric of the conditional form of Q11, rescaled in $\tau$, gives on each tangent space a Lorentzian
    interval with isometry group $\SO(3,1)$; its two coefficients are independent and fixed by no source.
    [H-cone] identifies the capacity load with the velocity of an inertial splitting.
    An inertial observer is a unit timelike splitting of this interval; changing observer is
    changing the splitting while preserving the interval.
    The unique proper orthochronous isometry at capacity load $\beta$ fixing the transverse directions is the Lorentz
    boost:
    \[
      \tau' = \gamma(\tau - \beta x), \qquad
      x'   = \gamma(x  - \beta\tau).
    \]
    Consequently, a rest length $\ell_0$ is measured as $\ell = \ell_0/\gamma$.

    The full Lorentz transformation, including length contraction, is thereby reconstructed
    from projective temporal ordering data, a supplied co-metric and [H-cone].
    The Born--Infeld capacity sphere provides $\beta$ and $\gamma$;
    it is not the Minkowski interval and does not carry the Lorentz group as an isometry.
